1. What is Hermes360

  • Hermes360 is a multi-tenant communications platform built and operated by Vocalcom.
  • Hermes is cloud-deployed: Vocalcom runs multiple cloud instances in parallel; each instance hosts a disjoint set of customers.
  • Hermes acts as the integration layer between our customers and your messaging platform.
  • Customers never talk to your platform directly — they interact with Hermes only. Hermes hides provider-specific identifiers, endpoints, and concepts from end customers entirely.
From your platform's point of view, Vocalcom is your customer — one provider account per integration. End customers do not hold provider credentials, portal access, or identifiers.

3. How we map our concepts onto your platform

Every send must be attributable across orthogonal dimensions. Your platform must accept all of them on the send path and echo them on every status event.

Hermes conceptWhat it representsWhat we need from your platformCardinality
Use case Outbound, Speed-to-lead, Notifications, Inbound… A use-case-level container / project / profile we can scope sends to ~5–10 per integration
Customer Hermes tenant (the billable account) Customer-level scope identifier (sub-account / entity / tag) 1 per Hermes customer, created eagerly on customer creation
Campaign Customer-owned send batch grouping A free-form campaign reference field on every send, echoed on every event 1 per campaign
Channel WHATSAPP today; SMS, RCS, Viber, email later Channel discriminator on the send payload selector
Send batch One API call that fans out to N recipients Bulk ID returned by send response 1 per send call
Single send (ContactAttempt) One recipient inside a batch Per-recipient message ID, ideally client-supplied for idempotency 1 per recipient
Outbound correlation token Hermes tag echoed on MO replies (matches reply to original send) Free-form field, returned in inbound MOs 1 per send
DLR correlation token Hermes tag echoed on delivery reports Free-form field, returned on DLR webhooks 1 per send
If your platform does not allow client-supplied message IDs or a stable echo-back field, Hermes cannot guarantee idempotency or reliably match DLRs back to the original send.

Multi-tenant isolation principles

  • Each Hermes customer is bound to a unique customer-scope identifier on your platform.
  • Phone numbers are customer-scoped: a number owned by Customer A cannot be used by Customer B (enforced by Hermes; your platform's tagging supports billing attribution).
  • A single number can be shared across multiple campaigns of the same customer.
  • Hermes resolves customer_id from the authenticated session, then queries provider-side resources or its own dispatch table filtered by that ID.

4.2 The setup steps

What Vocalcom does on Meta

  1. Create a Meta Business Portfolio in Vocalcom's name.
  2. Complete Meta Business Verification (legal docs, address, web presence).
  3. Register a Meta App in the Facebook for Developers console under the verified Portfolio.
  4. Add the WhatsApp Business product to the Meta App.
  5. Note the Meta App ID — Hermes passes it to the JS SDK when launching the signup widget.

What Vocalcom does with your platform

  1. Sign the provider contract; receive an API key + base URL.
  2. Receive your solution / configuration identifier — the value Meta uses to route a WABA to your platform when signup completes.
  3. (Coordinate with you, if required) link our Meta App to your solution identifier so signups launched under our App land on your platform.
  4. Configure webhook URLs for: delivery reports, inbound messages, signup status, template status.
  5. Store all of the above (App ID + solution ID + base URL + API key) in Hermes configuration.
Net result. Hermes holds three things bound together: Vocalcom Meta App ID + your solution identifier + your account API key. Every customer's WhatsApp signup flows under this triple. The customer never sees any of it.

5. Customer onboarding — WhatsApp number

5.1 What the customer must have

  • A Meta Business Manager in the customer's company name.
  • A phone number they own, capable of receiving SMS or voice. Hermes does not procure numbers from your inventory — customers bring their own MSISDN.
  • The number must not be active on the consumer WhatsApp app.
  • Admin rights on their Business Manager.
  • If the number already has a WABA on another provider — the two-step verification PIN for migration.

5.2 How the customer "uses your platform without knowing it"

  • Handshake 2 happens entirely inside Meta. The provider's solution ID we passed to the SDK is what makes Meta route the resulting WABA to your platform.
  • Handshake 3 is server-to-server between Hermes and your platform; the customer never sees it. Your platform binds the WABA to the customer scope we identify.
Customer experience: "I clicked Add Number, logged into Meta, my number is live." They have no way to tell which provider their messages flow through.

5.3 Failure modes Hermes surfaces back to the customer

  • Number in use on consumer WhatsApp — Meta refuses; customer must delete the consumer account.
  • Migration PIN wrong — customer must reset PIN at Meta or wait 7 days for automatic recovery.
  • Business verification required — customer must complete Meta Business Verification (2–5 business days).

6. Templates & sending

Two-phase flow: a one-off template registration / approval, then an unlimited number of sends. Free-form text is allowed inside the 24h session window; outside it, only an approved template will send.

  • Categories: AUTHENTICATION, UTILITY, MARKETING.
  • Status values (Meta-controlled): PENDING_APPROVAL, APPROVED, REJECTED, PAUSED, DISABLED.
  • Hard duplicates — same (WABA, name, language) → Hermes short-circuits with a 409 before calling you.
  • Soft duplicates — same body / structure under different names → UI warning, not blocked.
  • Language variants — same name + different language → allowed; one row per variant.
  • Body stored in Hermes DB for catalog browsing and dedup (templates are not PII).

6.3 What Hermes persists per send (per recipient)

  • MessageId (provider-supplied, unique) and BulkId (provider-supplied, indexed).
  • Destination (recipient MSISDN / WA business id).
  • Use-case scope, customer scope, campaign reference, channel, sender.
  • LatestStatusGroupName, LatestStatusName, error code, permanent flag.
  • CorrelationData, CallbackData (echoed-back tokens).
  • Retry chain: RetryCount, RetryOfMessageId.
  • Timestamps: SentAt, LastStatusRefreshAt, DeliveredAt, FinalizedAt.

Webhook delivery contract

  • One webhook URL per event family, configured on your side; Hermes supplies the URLs.
  • Every payload must carry the customer scope, use-case scope, and the original campaign reference + correlation tokens we attached on the send.
  • Signed payloads with HMAC or equivalent; published key-rotation procedure.
  • Documented retry policy: how many retries, with what backoff, until when.
  • Pull-based fallback: a reports / MDR endpoint Hermes can poll to drain anything missed at webhook time.
